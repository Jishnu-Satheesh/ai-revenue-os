# Market Monitoring workflow and experience audit

The current platform contains much of the machinery for branch-scoped research, but the production worker does not connect it to live retrieval and extraction. It also has no research-report product: Market Watch presents individual evidence cards and status history. Completing the requested experience requires changes to research inputs, collection depth, business-context assembly, report persistence, review, recurring work and the interface.

This audit and its interactive review draft were completed on 13 September 2026. Findings below describe the checked-out code. Proposed remedies and layouts are for discussion; they are not an approved implementation design. Hosted deployment state and a completed paid research run were not independently verified during this audit.

## Confirmed product requirements

- Start research for a business location, competitors and a freely described business objective or event.
- Brave supplies external evidence. Gemini may analyze that evidence without using its own search tool for this flow.
- Research runs in the background through Trigger.dev.
- Research investigates competitor performance signals, digital presence, marketplace presence, offers and positive/negative customer feedback, plus the local opportunity described in the brief.
- Strategy uses permitted Business Memory, business facts and channel evidence.
- The client reads a short, useful report in simple language inside Market Watch, with supporting detail available when needed. The first version uses simple English, as confirmed by the user.
- Each research produces a report and draft recommendations for review. Automatic addition to the existing feeds is not the selected workflow.
- After the client accepts selected items, the platform routes actionable advice to Recommendations and informational findings to Insights, based on each item’s type. This destination rule is confirmed by the user.
- The user chooses one-time or recurring research.
- Each location can have several independent research projects. Starting, editing or stopping an event project must not replace another project at that location.
- The report may include speculative competitor financial ranges even without published financial figures or a sourced calculation. It must clearly identify them as speculation and explain the signals, assumptions and reasoning used. Such ranges are advisory estimates, not recorded competitor results.
- Report review happens in a dialog using a readable custom brief, with a PDF download. The user confirmed this as the default reader.

Simple English and a readable brief with PDF download are confirmed for the first version. No numeric operating allowance or customer-facing completion-time promise is approved here.

## Candidate experience for discussion

Three approaches are possible. A settings-only refresh is smallest but does not deliver a project/report workflow. A separate research application is expansive and duplicates Growth Intelligence navigation. The proposed middle approach is to keep the four Growth Intelligence tabs and make research briefs and reports first-class within Insights & market.

### Independent projects at one location — confirmed direction

A location can have “National Day sales”, “Competitor monitoring” and another research project at the same time. These are separate assignments with separate histories. The confirmed requirement is independence; the following lifecycle details are proposed to make it reliable and understandable.

| Concept          | What the client sees                                                    | Proposed responsibility                                                                |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Location         | The outlet and local area the project concerns                          | Owns the canonical business location; research cannot silently edit it.                |
| Research project | A named question or ongoing watch                                       | Owns its objective, one-time/recurring choice, schedule and lifecycle.                 |
| Research brief   | The exact question, competitors, area and context reviewed before a run | An immutable revision; later edits apply explicitly to future work.                    |
| Research update  | One background attempt to investigate the reviewed brief                | Owns progress, coverage, cost and the pinned internal-context snapshot.                |
| Report           | A saved, readable answer for that update                                | Owns citations, assumptions, speculative estimates, draft advice and its review state. |

Creating a new project does not replace another project's brief or cancel another project's run. Editing a project while its research is running saves the new brief for the next run by default; any request to stop the active run is a separate, explicit action. The running result retains the brief it actually researched and can be labelled “Based on the previous brief” when needed.

**Pause monitoring** stops future scheduled starts for that project. **Stop this research** cancels its current update. **Archive project** removes it from the active list and stops new scheduled work while keeping accessible reports and review history. These labels must not be conflated with closing the dialog or deleting evidence. Pause/stop/archive affect only the addressed project.

Recurring updates should not overlap within one project. A manual refresh during an active run opens its progress instead of starting duplicate paid work. Separate projects may queue or run according to a fair organization allowance; the visible queue must explain waiting without inventing a completion percentage. A settings-only save must not restart research or spend money by implication.

The research list can show all projects across permitted locations, filtered by location and project status. Each row identifies its own topic and location. Status uses two separate concepts when necessary: “Monitoring paused” describes the project; “Report ready to review” describes its latest result. Pausing a schedule must not hide a finished report awaiting review.

The previous successful report stays visible during a new update or a failure. Every history entry opens its exact report and brief revision, even if the project's title, competitors or schedule later change. Changing the page's filter must not change the identity of an already opened report.

Shared competitor observations may reduce repeat collection across projects only when they remain current, geographically relevant, traceable and permitted for reuse. A new report still needs to answer its own question. Projects must not treat another project's speculative financial range as an observed competitor fact.

Two reports may suggest the same action. Acceptance should show an existing matching recommendation when appropriate and retain both supporting report links, rather than silently creating duplicate advice or overwriting an accepted action. The platform determines the destination by item type after the client accepts the selected items; this routing rule is now confirmed.

Acceptance cases added by this decision: two active projects on one branch; edits and cancellation isolated to one project; concurrent manual/scheduled starts deduplicated within a project; independent schedules; a failed update retaining the prior report; historical report links after a brief edit; report access restricted to the correct organization; no cross-project promotion of speculative estimates into facts.

### Starting research

Use **New research** as the action; reserve **Monitoring settings** for a recurring project's settings. A spacious dialog has a stable header, a scrolling body and persistent actions. On a phone it uses the available screen width and avoids side-by-side form fields.

The first section asks “What would you like to achieve?” with a multiline brief and an optional event/target date. A location field shows the chosen outlet and research area. Available business facts prefill the form; important missing facts are visible and editable within research scope.

Competitors are editable identity rows, with suggested competitors clearly marked as suggestions. The brief should show the categories the research intends to investigate, rather than making the client invent every search term. An expandable context section explains which business/channel periods and memory sources will inform the strategy.

The final section selects **One-time** or **Keep monitoring**. Recurring mode exposes cadence and an end date/stop condition. A short reviewed summary binds the exact brief before Start. This borrows the GOV.UK check-answers principle without copying its visual design. [W3]

Research starts only after the reviewed input is saved. Closing the dialog lets work continue. A report card shows the actual stage, and later opens the exact output. Estimated completion time should appear only after runtime measurements justify it.

### Insights & market

Keep the existing Growth Intelligence header and line tabs. Use a clearly labelled location filter and distinguish report dates from business-evidence periods.

Make **Market Watch** a primary report area: each row/card shows the business question, location, latest research date, one-time/recurring state, a short takeaway and **Review report**. Pending review and running work are easy to scan. History remains accessible without filling the page with all previous attempts.

Business insights retain a separate, clearly named section. Data gaps should name the missing business input and next action. Source details sit behind the statement they support. Avoid a wall of equally prominent evidence and status cards.

For recurring work, the report leads with changes since the previous successful run. If no material changes were found, say so; a new timestamp is not a new insight. Keep the prior report visible during refresh or failure.

The visual direction borrows Channels' restrained green actions, clear section spacing and honest coverage labels; Overview's clear attention area; and Campaign's complete proposal review and explicit status. It does not reuse illustrative business metrics or fictional competitor claims.

### Report review

The user selected an accessible readable brief in the dialog, with PDF download from the same structured report. The layout reflows for phone users; the PDF preserves the same content and citations for sharing. The installed PDF viewer dependency does not settle PDF generation, accessibility, permissions or report storage. Mozilla documents the separation between PDF parsing, display and viewer layers. [W4]

The report should open on a short brief: what matters, why it matters here, and suggested next steps. Optional detail contains competitor comparisons, offers/reviews, supporting business evidence, assumptions, unknowns and sources. This follows the separation between research, synthesis and export seen in Perplexity, and between ongoing feeds and organized intelligence seen in Feedly. These are product references, not promises to match their coverage or speed. [W5, W6]

Draft advice is reviewed in the context of the report. Accepting selected advice adds it to Recommendations; accepting informational findings adds them to Insights. The platform selects the destination from each item’s type, retaining report/version provenance and deduplication. Rejecting or saving for later does not publish anything. Campaign creation, spend, scheduling and external publication continue through their existing approval paths.

For accessibility, the dialog needs a meaningful title, predictable focus, contained keyboard navigation, Escape/Close behavior and focus restoration. Large reports should expose structured text rather than announcing the whole document as a dialog description. [W7]

## Research architecture choices to resolve

- **Work identity — confirmed:** independent research projects at each location. A project owns its brief revisions, schedule, runs, reports and review history. A new project must not replace the branch market profile in a way that cancels or changes another project. Shared market context can be reused only with explicit scope, provenance, freshness and permitted reuse.
- **Collection:** retain basic Brave Web Search as a discovery tool, assess Brave LLM Context for richer retrieved passages, and evaluate any additional page retrieval separately. Current LLM Context documentation describes extracted text, source metadata, token limits, freshness and local context; it does not promise full coverage of reviews, paywalled data or private competitor finances. [W1]
- **Research plan:** derive specific questions for competitor identity, presence, offers, reviews, observable performance and local/event demand. Assess coverage after each pass and conduct bounded follow-up searches where they can change the recommendation.
- **Business context:** build an organization/branch-scoped snapshot of permitted facts, goals, constraints, relevant channel measurements and memory. Keep private context out of public search queries. Internal context explains relevance; it does not establish external claims.
- **Report:** persist validated structured content and its evidence/brief version before marking it ready. Keep PDF generation as a separate render state so a PDF rendering failure need not lose the readable report.
- **Review:** record decisions against the exact report/advice version and promote selected items idempotently. Recheck source availability and material changes before later execution.
- **Recurring work:** retain snapshots, compare meaningful changes and preserve review history. Do not regenerate duplicate advice or claim a trend from incomparable observations.

## Remaining operating qualification

The core product choices are now answered: independent projects, one-time or recurring research, report/draft review before feed addition, clearly explained speculative estimates, a readable dialog with PDF download, simple English initially, and destination selection by accepted item type.

Research cost and completion time still need a measured provider canary. No numeric allowance, automatic spending increase or completion-time promise is approved here. The implementation plan must identify the applicable organization allowance and measure an end-to-end example before proposing a new operating limit. This qualification does not require choosing a different reader or reorganizing the four tabs.

## Verification required before implementation can be called complete

- A first-time operator can create a valid brief without an existing proposal; a viewer can read authorized reports but cannot start or accept work.
- Every chosen locality, topic and competitor reaches the saved brief and coverage record. Test non-Latin names, duplicate identities, long briefs, missing locality and conflicting event dates.
- A real, qualified Brave request returns useful bounded evidence; extraction and support review are connected; costs and failed/unknown attempts remain honest.
- The strategist demonstrably receives the permitted business context and cited external findings, with no cross-organization or wrong-branch material.
- A completed report answers the stated business question in simple language, including incomplete coverage and uncertainty. Compare the report against its sources, not only a JSON schema.
- Refresh, reload, offline return, timeout, cancellation, retry and duplicate delivery preserve the right report and run status. Lost dispatch and recurring scheduling are verified on the deployed worker.
- Report review, selected-advice promotion, feed links and later Campaign handoff preserve exact identity and separate approvals.
- PDF/custom reader, source links and exports obey report authorization and retention, and work on phone, keyboard and zoomed layouts.
