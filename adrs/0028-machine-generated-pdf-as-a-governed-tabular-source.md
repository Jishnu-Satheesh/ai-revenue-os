# ADR 0028: Machine-generated PDF as a governed tabular source

## Status

Proposed. Reverses the blanket PDF exclusion in `specs/018` section 4.3.

## Context

Release 1 excluded "PDFs as financial-data uploads" outright. That was the right
call against the risk it was written for: OCR over a scanned page produces
numbers nobody can trace, and a model transcribing a statement produces numbers
nobody can reproduce.

Profiling the pilot client's real exports on 2026-08-22 showed the exclusion
also blocks things that carry none of that risk:

- The offline store's profit and loss is available only as PDF. It is the sole
  source of food cost and packaging — the two inputs contribution margin has
  always been missing. It is generated by wkhtmltopdf, so it is a rendered HTML
  table with an intact text layer.
- Keeta's commission invoices and monthly statements are generated by iText.

None is a scan. Every figure already exists as selectable text at a known
position. Refusing them costs the client the only cost structure they have,
while protecting against a risk those files do not carry.

## Decision

A PDF is admitted as a governed tabular source **only where its numbers already
exist as text**.

- The parser adapter extracts a positioned text layer and reconstructs a cell
  grid. That grid enters the same contract, validation, projection, lineage, and
  reconciliation path a spreadsheet takes. Nothing downstream learns it came
  from a PDF.
- A PDF with no text layer, or one whose text layer yields no reconstructable
  grid, is a typed failure with an actionable message. There is no OCR, no image
  interpretation, and no fallback that guesses.
- No model reads a value out of a PDF. A model may propose the mapping over
  extracted structure under `specs/018` section 8.4, exactly as it may for a
  spreadsheet, and its proposal is inert until an owner or admin approves it.
- Where a PDF restates a figure a spreadsheet already carries for the same
  period, the spreadsheet is the source and the PDF is a **control document**:
  its stated totals are reconciled against the projected rows, and a mismatch
  fails the import rather than selecting a winner.

## Consequences

The offline channel becomes readable, and food cost stops being a number the
operator has to know about their own business.

Grid reconstruction from positioned text is genuinely harder than reading a
spreadsheet, and it is where this will break first: multi-line row labels,
columns separated by whitespace rather than delimiters, and bilingual
right-to-left documents all defeat naive column inference. The mitigation is
that a PDF is never trusted on its own — an approved contract states the
expected columns, and a control total has to reconcile before anything is
projected. A layout the extractor reads wrongly fails arithmetic rather than
silently producing a plausible number.

Because extraction is deterministic, the same file always yields the same grid,
so a PDF-sourced figure carries the same lineage and reproducibility guarantees
as a spreadsheet-sourced one. That is the property the original exclusion was
protecting, and it is preserved here by construction rather than by prohibition.

Bilingual right-to-left invoices remain the hardest case. They are also
redundant with Keeta's billing spreadsheet, so they are used as control
documents and are never a primary source.
