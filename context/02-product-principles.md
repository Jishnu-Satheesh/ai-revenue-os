# Product Principles

## 1. Outcome before automation

A workflow is valuable only when it changes a measurable business outcome, reduces meaningful cost, protects revenue, or increases decision quality.

## 2. Gross profit before gross revenue

Discount-heavy promotions can increase orders while destroying contribution margin. Opportunity scoring should consider commission, discount funding, ad spend, food cost, fulfillment cost, refunds, and operational capacity.

## 3. Goal-driven, not workflow-driven

Users define outcomes and constraints. The platform selects appropriate playbooks and actions. Workflows are implementation details, not the main user experience.

## 4. Human governance proportional to risk

Low-risk reversible actions may run automatically. High-spend, public, destructive, irreversible, regulated, or brand-sensitive actions require approval.

## 5. Explain every important decision

The system should show evidence, assumptions, expected impact, uncertainty, and policy reasoning. Confidence scores must not be presented as guarantees.

## 6. Learn from every action

Every action produces an outcome record, even when the action fails or is rejected. Rejections are useful feedback when reasons are captured.

## 7. Industry-neutral core, domain-rich packs

The core defines organizations, goals, events, opportunities, policies, runs, tools, memory, and measurement. Industry Packs define domain entities, signals, playbooks, and integrations.

## 8. Progressive data quality

The system must provide value with incomplete data while clearly distinguishing verified, imported, inferred, stale, and missing information.

## 9. Capability abstraction

Workers request capabilities such as `publish_ad_campaign` or `send_whatsapp_message`, not vendor-specific implementation details. Tool adapters map capabilities to providers.

## 10. Durable auditability

The system must reconstruct who or what decided, approved, executed, changed, and measured every sensitive action.
